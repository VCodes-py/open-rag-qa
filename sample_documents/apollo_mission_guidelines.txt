APOLLO MISSION LOGISTICS AND FLIGHT MANUAL SPECIFICATIONS
Document Classification: Historical Engineering Archive (NASA Apollo Program)

SECTION 1: SATURN V LAUNCH VEHICLE ARCHITECTURE
The Saturn V three-stage rocket stood 363 feet (110.6 meters) tall and possessed a total launch mass of approximately 6,540,000 pounds (2,970,000 kilograms) fully fueled.

1.1 S-IC First Stage:
- Propulsion: 5 Rocketdyne F-1 engines burning RP-1 (refined kerosene) and liquid oxygen (LOX).
- Total Thrust: 7,500,000 pounds-force (33,400 kilonewtons) at sea level.
- Burn Duration: Approximately 168 seconds, accelerating the vehicle to Mach 7 (approx. 6,100 mph / 9,800 km/h) before staging at an altitude of 42 miles (68 kilometers).

1.2 S-II Second Stage:
- Propulsion: 5 Rocketdyne J-2 engines utilizing cryogenic liquid hydrogen (LH2) and liquid oxygen (LOX).
- Total Thrust: 1,000,000 pounds-force (4,400 kilonewtons).
- Burn Duration: Approximately 360 seconds, lifting the Apollo stack into near-orbital velocity.

1.3 S-IVB Third Stage:
- Propulsion: Single Rocketdyne J-2 engine capable of restart in vacuum.
- Primary Tasks: Inserted spacecraft into circular 115-nautical-mile Earth Parking Orbit, followed by the critical Trans-Lunar Injection (TLI) burn of approximately 350 seconds.

SECTION 2: APOLLO GUIDANCE COMPUTER (AGC)
Developed by the MIT Instrumentation Laboratory, the AGC utilized pioneering silicon integrated circuits.
- Processor Clock Speed: 1.024 MHz.
- Memory: 2,048 words of 16-bit RAM (magnetic core memory) and 36,864 words of ROM (core rope memory).
- User Interface: Display and Keyboard unit known as the DSKy ("Dis-Key"), featuring numeric displays and Verb/Noun operation registers.
- Error Handling: During Apollo 11's lunar descent, Radar Priority overload triggered Program Alarms 1201 and 1202. The real-time executive software designed by Margaret Hamilton dropped low-priority radar tasks to maintain guidance thrust calculations.

SECTION 3: LUNAR MODULE (LM) LANDING TELEMETRY
- Ascent Stage Mass: 4,700 kilograms containing crew cabin, environmental controls, and RCS thrusters.
- Descent Stage Engine: Throttleable between 10% and 60% thrust (1,050 to 9,850 lbf) to enable fine pitch-over hover and terminal touchdown.
- Surface Stay Contingency: Oxygen supply certified for up to 75 hours in nominal lunar EVA configurations.
